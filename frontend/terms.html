6. How encryption works, and what it cannot do

When end-to-end encryption is on (the default), text messages and documents are encrypted in your browser before they leave your device and can only be opened by you and the person you are talking to. We do not hold the keys. This is a strong protection, but you should understand its limits:

  • Lost keys cannot be recovered. If you clear your browser data, change device, or lose a manual key or backup password, old messages may become unreadable. We cannot help, because we never had your keys.
  • Your device and the other person’s device are the weak points. If either has malware, a keylogger, a malicious browser extension, or is used by someone else, the messages can be read there. Screenshots and copies are always possible.
  • Text, documents, photos, voice notes and videos are end-to-end encrypted when end-to-end encryption is on. Items sent before this protection existed, or with encryption switched off, are not end-to-end encrypted: they are protected in transit (HTTPS) and by access controls on our servers, but our systems can technically access them.
  • Groups: a group has its own key, made on a member’s device and given to each member encrypted for them. Everyone who is in the group can read what is sent while they are in it, and can copy or share it. We cannot read group messages. When someone leaves or is removed, a new key is made so they cannot read what is sent afterwards, but they keep anything they already received. If a person joins, they cannot read what was sent before they joined.
  • If you turn encryption off for a chat, or use an older password mode, that chat is not end-to-end encrypted. Group chats cannot be sent unencrypted.
  • Some information is not hidden: who talks to whom, when, file sizes, who is in each group and the group name, your account details, and IP addresses. See the Privacy Policy.
  • Voice typing is done by your browser’s speech service (in Chrome and Edge, by Google or Microsoft), not by us. Words you say are not covered by our encryption until they are typed.
  • Shared passwords and keys are your responsibility. A weak password protects very little, and anyone who knows it can decrypt what it protects.
  • We cannot scan encrypted documents for viruses. We only allow a short list of common file types and you should only open files from people you trust.
  • The emoji view is a way of displaying encrypted data. It is not what protects your message. The encryption does.
